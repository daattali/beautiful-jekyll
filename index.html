Aarushi Sehgal

I build statistical tools that find hidden mutations in repetitive DNA, and I’m working to carry them to patients.

I’m a PhD candidate in Computer Science & Engineering at UC San Diego, advised by Prof. Melissa Gymrek. I develop methods to detect somatic mosaicism at short tandem repeats (mutation present in only subset of individual's cell population) and to understand their role in cancer and neurological disease across diverse populations. I created prancSTR, the first hypothesis-testing method for finding mosaic repeats without a matched control sample.

Next, I want to take this work the last mile: starting from patients rather than sequences, beginning with RFC1 repeat expansions in CANVAS, an ataxia that often goes undiagnosed for years. Along the way I’ve built pangenome tools with collaborators in Africa, worked on cancer genomics at Exact Sciences, and spent time across six hospital specialties to see how clinical decisions are really made.

I grew up in India and studied computer science at NIT Hamirpur in the Himalayan foothills, with a research summer at the University of British Columbia along the way.

  • Bioinformatics

    Genome-wide detection of somatic mosaicism at short tandem repeats

    Sehgal A., et al.

    Bioinformatics, 2024

    In brief

    Post-zygotic mutations at Short Tandem Repeats create genetically distinct subsets of cells within an individual, giving rise to somatic mosaicism. No reliable statistical method existed for detecting these mutations at genome-wide scale. We introduce prancSTR, which models sequencing reads as a mixture to estimate mosaic fractions without a matched control, and use it on 1000 Genomes data to reveal population-specific patterns of mosaic mutation.

  • Genome Biology

    LongTR: genome-wide profiling of genetic variation at tandem repeats from long reads

    Ziaei Jam H., et al.

    Genome Biology, 2024

    In brief

    Long reads can span tandem repeats that short reads can’t. LongTR genotypes tandem repeats genome-wide from long-read sequencing and reports full allele sequences, not just lengths, opening long and complex repeats to population-scale study.
